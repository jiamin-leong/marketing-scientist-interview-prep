⭐ The only 10 things to memorise
Refreshed against tonight's priority order. Learn these ten sentences cold — everything below is recognition-only.
1Ask what decision the test informs before naming a metric or a method. "Is advertising working?" and "is this add-on worth it?" are different questions with different controls.
2The control group's definition changes with the question. Holdout (suppressed from the campaign) answers "does advertising work." BAU-only vs. BAU+add-on answers "is the add-on worth it."
3Ghost / PSA ads preserve budget + auction parity for a holdout test — the confound isn't just attention, it's that an unspent control budget changes auction dynamics for treatment too.
4Add-on budget must be truly incremental, not carved from the existing BAU pool — otherwise you've reallocated spend, not added it, and BAU delivery differs across arms.
5Report incremental ROAS = (treatment outcome − control outcome) ÷ incremental spend. Raw totals are trivially higher in treatment when you spent more — that's not informative.
6Never reallocate budget across arms mid-flight. That breaks randomisation. Reallocation is the decision you make after the read-out, using its result.
7Split driver analysis into product-side vs. auction-side before naming a model. They point to different fixes — and watch for endogeneity on the auction side: a bid can rise because performance is already good, not the other way round.
8Regression for the Sales narrative; random forest/GBM for ranked drivers. Always name the tradeoff (interpretability vs. handling non-linearity) and caveat correlation vs. causation.
9Every case: Story → Goal → Solution → Measurement proof → Results → Recommendation. Name real Snap products (Smart Campaign Solutions, Snap Pixel, Conversions API, Conversion Lift, Dynamic Product Ads, Unified Attribution) — not Meta's.
10Two-tier China framing. Scale/self-serve for the broad book ("drive scaled adoption"); a structured learning agenda for top advertisers. Either way, your driver-analysis output feeds the vertical marketing science lead's hypothesis queue — you're not the unilateral decision-maker for every vertical.
Two habits that carry every answer: open by naming the exact decision a test/analysis informs before naming a method · close with a recommendation framed as incremental ROI, not just a lift percentage.
🧪 Experimentation & incrementality — top priority
The core technical seam under any case — how you know a lift number is real.
Say this"The control group's definition depends on the question. If we're asking whether advertising works at all, control is a holdout suppressed from the campaign — and we protect that with ghost ads so budget and auction dynamics stay equal. If we're asking whether a new format is worth the extra spend, control is BAU-only and treatment is BAU plus the add-on — there the asymmetry is the point, so I'd evaluate it on incremental ROAS, not raw outcome."
If probed → randomisation unit (user vs. geo) · why not simply omit ads to control (auction/attention confound) · why not reallocate mid-test (breaks randomisation) · what makes a lift number defensible to a skeptical advertiser.

Design decisions, one at a time

Randomisation unitDefault user-level (hashed device/user ID) when the platform controls per-user delivery. Fall back to geo-level when you can't isolate at the user level — new channel entirely, offline/halo effects, or risk of spillover between a treated and a control user (social/viral products).
Ghost / PSA adsControl still gets served a neutral placeholder ad instead of nothing. Preserves attention parity (both groups had a slot filled) AND budget/auction parity (control still spends, still competes) — so any measured difference is attributable to ad content, not delivery mechanics.
Holdout test (ads vs. no ads)Answers: does advertising drive any incremental effect at all. Budget symmetry across arms is the thing you protect.
Add-on test (BAU vs. BAU+format)Answers: is this specific new element worth its incremental spend. Budget asymmetry is expected — what you protect instead is that the add-on's budget is genuinely incremental, not reallocated from BAU, and that its impressions are additive reach rather than cannibalising BAU delivery on the same users.
Mid-flight budget shiftsNever reallocate between arms while a test is running — an adaptive/bandit-style shift breaks random assignment and invalidates the causal read. Smart/auto-bidding that only "sees" conversions in treatment will also self-select toward easy converters — freeze targeting + bid strategy identically across arms for the test duration.
Power / MDEMinimum Detectable Effect shrinks with more users, longer duration, and lower outcome variance. Sanity-check on the spot: bigger baseline variance (e.g. high-value whale spenders) needs a larger sample or a capped/winsorised metric to stay powered.
Underpowered vs. true nullA flat result could mean "no effect" or "not enough data to tell." Always check the CI/MDE before declaring a null — especially when it contradicts a plausible mechanism.
Learning agendaA structured, prioritised testing roadmap for a top advertiser — tests sequenced so each one builds on the last (e.g. frequency cap → creative refresh → bid strategy), rather than ad hoc one-off tests. JD-named term, scoped specifically to top advertisers — distinct from the "scaled/self-serve" framing that applies to the broader book. Programmatic here means systematic and repeatable, not automated.
🎯 Driver analysis & stats fundamentals — high priority
Condensed — the underlying math, ready if they go one layer technical.
Say this"When I diagnose a campaign, I'd split drivers into two lenses — product-side (what's being shown: creative, targeting, catalog quality) and auction-side (how it's being bought: bid strategy, pacing, delivery competition) — since they point to different fixes. My output is a prioritised, evidence-backed hypothesis list I'd hand to the vertical marketing science lead, not necessarily every hypothesis tested by me end-to-end."
Product-side driversCreative/format quality, targeting/audience mix, catalog data quality (Dynamic Product Ads), frequency/fatigue, placement mix — what's being shown, to whom.
Auction-side driversBid strategy, budget pacing, delivery/competition density, CPM trends, Smart Campaign Solutions optimisation settings — how it's being bought.
Feeding vertical leadsThe JD frames this role as producing inputs for vertical marketing science leaders (gaming, e-commerce) to hypothesis-test — this role is horizontal/China-wide, verticals own deeper strategy. Frame driver-analysis output as a prioritised hypothesis list, not a unilateral decision.
Regression vs. random forestRegression when you need interpretable, signed coefficients to build a Sales narrative ("+1pt CTR associates with +X ROAS"). Random forest / GBM when you want ranked feature importance and can tolerate less interpretability, or the relationship is non-linear / has interactions. Both are associational — flag that top drivers should be validated with a test, not treated as causal on sight.
MulticollinearityCorrelated predictors (e.g. impressions and reach) make individual coefficients unstable/hard to interpret even if the overall model fits well. Check VIF, or just reason about which channels move together.
p-value / CI / power (recap)p-value = P(data this extreme | null true). 95% CI excludes 0 ⇔ significant at α=0.05. Power = P(detect a real effect) = 1−β, driven up by more n, bigger true effect, lower variance, higher α.
CLT on skewed ad metricsRevenue/user or ROAS per campaign is heavily skewed (zero-inflated + a long tail of whales), but you're doing inference on the mean, so CLT still makes z/t-tests valid at scale. The real risk is power (huge variance → large MDE) and a single whale flipping the result — fix with capping/winsorising, decomposing into rate × value-given-conversion, or CUPED on pre-period spend.
Optional stopping / peekingChecking a live test and stopping the moment p<0.05 inflates the true false-positive rate — predefine sample size/duration, or use sequential testing if you must monitor live.
Regression diagnostics — the natural follow-up once you've named a driver. Don't wait to be asked; volunteering one of these on a real regression answer is a strong signal.
R² / adjusted R²Share of variance explained. Not the goal — a high R² on the same data you fit on says nothing about generalisation. Adjusted R² penalises adding useless predictors; prefer it when comparing models with different predictor counts.
Residual diagnosticsPlot residuals vs. fitted values — a pattern (fan shape, curve) signals heteroscedasticity or a missed non-linearity, both of which bias your standard errors even if the coefficient estimate itself is fine.
Coefficient significanceA "driver" should clear its own p-value/CI, not just have the right sign — check each coefficient's SE, not just the model's overall R².
Outliers / influential pointsA handful of whale advertisers or campaigns can dominate a regression — check leverage / Cook's distance, not just eyeball the scatterplot. Ties back to the CLT/whale problem above.
⚠️ Endogeneity / reverse causalityThe sharpest trap for auction-side drivers specifically: bid amount often rises because a campaign is already performing well (advertiser/algorithm reacting to success), not the other way round. Regressing performance on bid without accounting for this direction gets the causal story backwards. Flag it, and reach for the same toolkit as the experimentation section — a randomised bid-strategy test, not another regression — to actually resolve it.
Omitted variable biasA missing confounder (seasonality, a competitor's campaign, a platform-wide auction shift) that affects both the driver and the outcome will bias the coefficient on the driver you did include. Name a plausible omitted variable before trusting a coefficient.
Example hypotheses — concrete enough to name live, structured by the product-side/auction-side split above. Format: Hypothesis → what you'd check → vertical note where relevant.

Product-side hypotheses (what's being shown)

Creative fatigueCTR decays as the same users see the same ad repeatedly. Check: frequency vs. CTR-decay curve.
Format mismatchA static image underperforming vs. AR Lens/video for an awareness-stage objective, or vice versa for a direct-response objective. Check: format-level CTR/IPM cut by campaign objective.
Catalog data qualityStale pricing or out-of-stock items in Dynamic Product Ads. Check: feed error/out-of-stock rate vs. conversion rate. E-commerce-specific.
Targeting too broad or too narrowLookalike seed too small, or targeting overlapping with the existing installed base (self-cannibalisation). Check: audience overlap with the current user base, lookalike seed size. Self-cannibalisation is especially relevant for gaming UA.
Tracking/measurement artifact, not a real product problemSnap Pixel/SDK event misfiring reads as "poor performance" when it's actually a broken conversion signal. Check: event-fire rate vs. expected volume — rule this out first, it's the "real vs. artifact" check.
Landing/store-listing drop-offClick-to-install gap separate from ad quality. Check: click-to-store-page rate vs. store-page-to-install rate, to isolate ad vs. app-store-listing. Gaming-specific.

Auction-side hypotheses (how it's being bought)

Bid strategy misaligned with the true objectiveOptimising for install when LTV is the real goal. Check: optimisation event vs. stated business objective.
Budget pacing frontloadingBudget exhausted early in the day, missing high-value auction windows later. Check: hourly spend-pacing curve vs. conversion-rate-by-hour.
Auction competition density spikeCPMs rising because of a seasonal/competitive surge (e.g. other advertisers ramping for a shopping festival), not anything the advertiser did. Check: CPM trend vs. category-wide spend, not just the campaign's own numbers.
Smart Campaign Solutions in learning phaseDelivery/CPA unstable early on. Check: days-since-launch and conversion volume vs. the platform's stabilisation threshold.
Bid cap too conservativeLosing high-value auctions because the cap is set below true value. Check: win-rate vs. bid cap, lost-auction volume.
EndogeneityBid raised because performance was already good, not the cause of it. Check: whether the bid change precedes or follows the performance shift in the time series.

🗄️ SQL quick-hits — short refresher
JD explicitly names SQL across large datasets — expect a live or take-home query on campaign/auction data.

Ad-data patterns

CTR / CVR / ROAS by segmentSUM(clicks)*1.0/NULLIF(SUM(impressions),0) — always cast to float and guard the denominator with NULLIF, never raw integer division.
Week-over-week / period-over-periodLAG(metric) OVER (PARTITION BY campaign_id ORDER BY week) — don't self-join when a window function does it in one pass.
Top-N per advertiser/campaignRANK() / ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY advertiser_id ORDER BY spend DESC).
⚠️ LEFT JOIN + WHEREA filter on the right table in WHERE silently turns a LEFT JOIN into an INNER JOIN — put the filter in the ON clause.
Incremental outcomeTreatment vs. control aggregates joined on test arm, then (treatment_outcome - control_outcome) / incremental_spend computed in the final SELECT — say this formula out loud if asked to write the lift query.

📈 Measurement stack — MMM, lift, brand lift, attribution — background
Know when each tool answers the question, not just its definition. Four tools, not three — brand lift is the upper-funnel counterpart to Conversion Lift, not a variant of it.
Say this"MMM, Conversion Lift, Brand Lift, and attribution answer different questions on different parts of the funnel. MMM gives a privacy-resilient, aggregate read across channels with some lag. Conversion Lift and Brand Lift share the same exposed-vs-control causal design — the difference is the outcome: Conversion Lift measures a logged behavioural event, Brand Lift measures a survey response on awareness, recall, or intent. A mature program uses all of them: MMM for cross-channel budget allocation, lift tests to validate the MMM's elasticities, and Brand Lift specifically for upper-funnel/awareness spend where there's no conversion event to measure yet."
If probed → Brand Lift's outcome is a survey response, not a logged event, so it needs a research-partner-style survey pipeline (exposed users and a matched control get polled, typically via a third-party panel) rather than pixel/SDK tracking · response rates are low, so the test needs a bigger reach/longer duration to power than a typical Conversion Lift · standard metrics: aided/unaided ad recall, brand awareness, favourability, message association, purchase intent.
MMM (media mix modeling)Regresses aggregate outcome (revenue/conversions) on aggregate spend by channel over time. Uses adstock (carryover — an impression's effect decays over following days/weeks, not instantly) and saturation curves (diminishing returns as spend in a channel rises). Privacy-resilient (no user-level tracking needed), but lagged and needs enough historical variation in spend to identify channel effects — collinear budgets (channels always moving together) make coefficients unstable.
Conversion Lift (incrementality)User- or geo-level randomised holdout, platform-run. Gives a causal, granular answer for one campaign on a behavioural outcome (purchase, install, sign-up), but needs meaningful spend/inventory to be powered, and doesn't automatically generalise to other campaigns or time periods.
Brand Lift surveysSame exposed-vs-control randomised design as Conversion Lift, but the outcome is a survey response from a matched exposed and control panel, not a logged conversion — measures aided/unaided ad recall, brand awareness, favourability, message association, purchase intent. Used for upper-funnel/awareness campaigns (AR Lens, video, Sponsored Snappables) where there's no conversion event yet to causally test on. Needs bigger reach and longer duration to power, since survey response rates are far lower than conversion rates, and typically runs through a third-party research/panel partner rather than pixel-based tracking.
MTA / Unified AttributionRules-based or algorithmic credit assignment across touchpoints (last-click, linear, data-driven). Granular and real-time, but breaks under privacy changes (iOS ATT, cookie deprecation) since it depends on user-level tracking, and it's correlational, not causal — it describes the path to conversion, not what would've happened without the ad.
Multi-study meta-analysisPool effect sizes across many advertiser-level lift tests (e.g. by vertical — gaming, e-commerce) to build a generalised elasticity / diminishing-returns curve. Method: hierarchical/mixed-effects model pooling studies, weighting by each test's power/precision, checking for effect heterogeneity before pooling rather than assuming one true effect.
Which to lead withConversion Lift is your credibility instrument for performance/lower-funnel spend. Brand Lift is the equivalent for awareness/upper-funnel spend — same logic, different outcome variable. MMM is your scaling instrument for budget allocation across many advertisers/channels at once. Frame it as "the outcome variable changes with the funnel stage," not as picking one tool.

📊 Phrasing % gains & numbers
Two traps in this section → percentage points vs. percent — a move from 10% to 12% is "增长了2个百分点" (up 2 percentage points), NOT "增长了百分之二十" (up 20%, which is the relative change) — mixing these is the single most common stats-communication error, in English or Mandarin. And the "倍" counting convention — "增长了一倍" means increased by 100% (result = 2× original), not "increased to 1×." Say "是原来的三倍" (is 3× the original) when you mean the new total, and "增长了两倍" (increased by 2×, i.e. +200%) when you mean the change — they are not interchangeable. Also note: 百分之 always comes before the number in Mandarin ("百分之十二" = twelve percent) — the reverse order of English "12%."
Increased by X%增长了百分之 X / 提升了百分之 X zēngzhǎng le bǎifēnzhī X / tíshēng le bǎifēnzhī X
"Revenue increased by 20% this quarter." — 这个季度收入增长了百分之二十。(Zhège jìdù shōurù zēngzhǎng le bǎifēnzhī èrshí.)
